What a skeptic asks

The hard questions, answered plainly

Does it find the right source?

Not as often as keyword search. On our 50 test searches, ordinary keyword search put the right source in its top ten for 82% of them; TRACE and exact search by meaning each reached 52%. Our 0.9985 measures how much of exact vector search's answer TRACE kept, not answer quality.

Does a matching fingerprint prove nothing relevant was skipped?

No. A matching fingerprint proves the passage you were shown is the one that was stored, with its article and position. Whether the shortcut skipped something relevant is a different question, and the comparison with exact search measures it: 0.9985 of exact search's top ten kept.

Is 0.79% the whole cost?

No. It counts passage-scoring work only. Choosing the groups, building them and the end-to-end time are separate costs: a TRACE search took 1.2 s against 88 ms for an exact scan, and keyword search took 6 ms.

Is the saving from self-organization, or from ordinary grouping?

We have not separated the two inside TRACE's own engine. The groups come from an ordinary sorting step, and we measured how ordered they are, not how they formed. In a simple stand-in (not TRACE) at the same 0.8% of passages, our groups found nearly all of the true best matches, arrival order alone found 92% and random groups about a third. The order we measured shows up at every size we tested (10,000 to 200,000 passages, a flat band with no trend), and we do not claim it improves with scale.

Does it work on public data?

TRACE has not been run there. A stand-in of ours gave dataset-specific results: FiQA about 95% at 7% scored, SciFact about 95% at 24%, NFCorpus 90% at 28% and 97% at 55%.
