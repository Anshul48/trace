TRACE is search that shows its work: it finds nearly the same passages as exact meaning-based search while looking at under 1% of the library, and every result comes with a checkable record. A research prototype over one sealed library, measured by us; no outside party has checked it yet.

Step 1. Clone

Working today

About 63 MB of packed Git data to download, about 74 MB checked out. Needs git.

git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/Anshul48/trace && cd trace

The clone holds the explorer, the only shipped dataset (the 20k sample, the first 88 documents of our source text: 88 documents, 20,030 tokens, 144 passages with vectors), and the records of our runs. There is nothing else to fetch.

Step 2. Run

Working today

Seconds. Needs Python 3 only. Downloads nothing.

python3 run_explorer.py --dataset 20k

It serves the explorer from your clone and opens it in your browser on the recorded-search replay page: a replay of 144 real searches TRACE answered on our own server, each result with a checkable record. It prints the explorer address, the Inside TRACE address, and Dataset: 20k prefix shipped in this repo. Press Ctrl+C to stop. On a machine with no browser, add --no-browser and open the printed address yourself.

Step 4. Look inside

Working today, our server

A few minutes. In the explorer.

Open the Inside TRACE address the server printed and switch to the Library tab: 1,430 real sample passages in 884 real groups across the 4 library parts. Pick a recorded question and watch it light its top 10 while the counter runs to the recorded aggregate (about 1,600 of 203,137 rows on one part, 0.79%). What this does not show

The two commands above are real, but they are the on-ramp, not the whole story. The limits, plainly:

  • Only the 20k prefix ships. --download 10m and --download 100m stop with exit 2 and say no such kit exists. A download for the whole library is planned, so there is no size or hardware list yet. Planned
  • There is no live service. The public backend is switched off: it failed a first test with many people using it at once. A later fix passed a local retest, but it stays off. Off
  • The headline result is ours, on our library, over 50 test searches. Scoring 0.79% of rows on one of four nearly equal library parts while keeping 0.9985 of exact meaning-based search's top ten (about 127 times less passage-scoring work; choosing and building the groups are separate costs). The sealed library holds 812,545 passages and 99,891,846 tokens, 0.11% short of the 100M target. For our library and our method only; not reproduced on public data. Measured by us Planned
  • Ordinary keyword search did better on this test. On the 50 test searches it put the right source passage in its top ten for 82% of them; TRACE did for 52%, and exact search by meaning also got 52%, so the gap is meaning versus keywords, not our shortcut. On a stricter score that also rewards putting the source first, 0.74 against 0.42. Keyword search also took less search-stage time (6 ms against our 1.2 s; the recorded setups differ, and we make no speed claim). TRACE is meaning-based search you can verify, not a keyword-search replacement.
  • The measured search is vector search only, with no keyword part combined in. The small 20k run runs the earlier frozen engine's protocol on a small sample; it does not reproduce the measured library's vector-only search; its numbers are not the sealed-library numbers.
  • Earlier, larger tests are ours and separate. The 310.5M and 504.5M-token builds ran on a machine that has since been shut down, so only their records exist; the 1.53B-token item was a separate group-picking test; we have not run a billion tokens through TRACE. None of them is the sealed-library result. The earlier tests, with every caveat.
