TRACE: a checkable memory store for coding agents

Nobody files the passages. TRACE lets them form their own groups by meaning, so developers and researchers can find the context their agents need. A question opens only the groups most likely to hold an answer, and every answer shows the page it came from. TRACE is a research prototype over one sealed library, and every number here has its limits written down.

The idea in three steps

Nobody files the books. A question walks to a few shelves. Every answer shows its page.

1. It sorts itself

Measured by us

Passages that mean alike end up on the same shelf, and no one adds labels. The order comes from the passages.

Passages that mean alike land on the same shelf, with no labels added.The grouping is measured in the evidence pages, with its limits. 50 test searches, our library and method.

2. A question wakes a part

Measured by us

A question is compared with the shelves first, and only the shelves most likely to hold the answer are searched.

0.79%of each of four nearly equal parts is scored per searchPassage-scoring work only (choosing the shelves and building them are separate costs), measured on each part; 99.85% of exact search's top ten kept, averaged across the parts. 50 test searches, our library and method.

3. Every answer has a record

Working today

Each result shows which article it came from, where in it, and a fingerprint your browser re-computes.

What TRACE is not.
