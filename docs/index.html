Search that shows its work

TRACE finds nearly the same passages as exact, meaning-based search while looking at under 1% of the library, and every result comes with a checkable record of where it came from.

Meaning-based means it matches what your question means, not only the words you typed.

  • Try the explorerPick a question, read the ranked passages, and check a record yourself in your browser.
  • Watch the demosShort films you can play right here, with captions and a written copy of the words.
  • Read the codeOpen source (Apache-2.0) on GitHub.

How it works, in three steps

  1. 1

    Ask in plain words

    Type a question, or pick one of the recorded ones.

  2. 2

    Get matching passages

    TRACE looks only inside the groups of passages most likely to hold the answer, under 1% of the library.

  3. 3

    Check the record

    Each result names its article and where in it, with a fingerprint of the exact text. Your browser can work the fingerprint out again.

Honest limits

  • Replay, not a live service. The explorer plays back real searches we recorded on our own server. Nothing is searching live behind the page, and the public live service is switched off.
  • Keyword search did better on our test. On our 50 test searches, ordinary keyword search put the right passage in its top ten for 82% of them. TRACE did for 52%, and so did exact meaning-based search. TRACE is for meaning-based search you can verify, not a replacement for keyword search.
  • Small test, not checked by anyone outside. 50 test searches on one library of about 800 thousand Wikipedia passages. We did not run the public search benchmarks.
  • No run-it-yourself kit yet. A download to run it on your own computer is planned, not built.

Every number and limit, with its scope, is in the explorer under “What this shows”.
