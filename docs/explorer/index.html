Self-Organizing Continuous Memory & Retrieval

TRACE is an experimental research retrieval architecture that organizes continuous document streams into an overlapping multi-tier directed acyclic graph (DAG) — without global clustering recomputation. It enables guided branch-descent retrieval that concentrates scoring compute on high-affinity clusters, achieving 0.73 recall@10 under a prototype evaluation protocol (all-sources mode; compare both measured modes, including 0.4758 in leave-source-out mode), without broad semantic IR benchmark claims.

3-Step Mechanism: Ingest • Centroid Placement • Bounded Routing Expand ↓
STEP 1
Ingest & Atomization
Documents → Sequence Atoms

Streaming text documents are tokenized and partitioned into contiguous lexical/dense atoms with sequence preservation, appending into a durable write-ahead ledger.

STEP 2
Centroid Placement & DAG
Overlapping Leaves & Parents

Atoms attach to leaf holons via centroid similarity (decide_leaf); leaves group beneath parent holons (decide_parent) forming an overlapping DAG (4,370 leaves share multiple parents; 9,540 atoms belong to multiple leaves).

STEP 3
Guided Query Routing
Bounded Branch Descent

Queries score 3,711 parent centroids, select top branches, and descend candidate child leaves to rank atoms, concentrating compute on high-likelihood branches under bounded row budgets.

Negative Findings & Bottlenecks (Research Transparency)

  • Drain Scaling Bottleneck (100M Not Qualified): Background drain is the primary operational scaling bottleneck toward 100M tokens (100M not qualified). In the measured 10M qualification run, remaining drain under reader traffic consumed 4,865.6 s (37.5% of total qualification wall) and initial drain consumed 1,780.5 s (13.7%).
  • Substantial Repair Cost: repair_to_zero consumed 2,234.4 s (17.2% of total qualification wall) across 5 bounded iterations to drain residual debt from 5,314 to 0.
  • Reader Traffic Overhead: Background publication and drain cycles operated under concurrent reader traffic conditions (2 active reader threads executing 31,625 total queries), incurring substantial synchronization and contention overhead during drain execution.
  • Cosine Cache Ineffective: Software cosine caching yielded no measurable throughput improvement under reader traffic conditions; caching did not resolve the background drain scaling bottleneck.
