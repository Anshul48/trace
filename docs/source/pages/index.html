A memory that organizes itself.

TRACE organizes a 100M library by meaning, so developers and researchers can find the context their agents need. Passages form their own groups, and a question opens only the groups most likely to hold an answer.

The idea in three beats

Nobody files the books. A question walks to a few shelves. Every answer shows its page.

1. It sorts itself

{{badge:groups-hold-matches}}

Passages that mean alike end up on the same shelf, and no one adds labels. The order comes from the passages.

about 7shelves hold a search's ten best matchesAt most 10 are possible; groups made at random would give 10. 50 test searches, our library and method.

2. A question wakes a part

{{badge:sparse-search}}

A question is compared with the shelves first, and only the shelves most likely to hold the answer are searched.

0.79%of one of four equal parts is scored per searchPassage-scoring work only (choosing the shelves and building them are separate costs), and 99.85% of exact search's top ten kept. 50 test searches, our library and method.

3. Every answer has a record

{{badge:record-check}}

Each result shows which article it came from, where in it, and a fingerprint your browser re-computes.

144real recorded searches you can openOur own re-run matched on all claims. No outside party has checked it. Open the explorer.

What TRACE is not.

Not a keyword-search replacement: ordinary keyword search found the right source more often than TRACE did. Not faster: a plain scan answers sooner. Not checked by anyone outside our team.
